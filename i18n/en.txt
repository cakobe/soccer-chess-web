28|01 — Rules
29|The game is built on a single loop: kick, decide who has the ball, move round, kick again. Moves test your mind, kicks test your skill.
30|The Pitch
31|The game is played on a football pitch divided into squares. Pieces are placed on the squares and distances are measured by counting squares.
32|Squares
33|Columns
35|Rows in each half
36|The coordinates are written so that each player can read them from their own side. When describing a position or asking an AI, you can refer to squares by these letters and numbers.
37|Move straight, count diagonally
38|Player pieces move only forwards, backwards, left and right; no piece can move diagonally. However, when deciding who has the ball, diagonal squares are also counted when measuring the distance from a player to the ball.
39|Move round
40|Before the match, both sides agree on how many move rounds to play with. With 1 round, each team makes one move before every kick: skill comes to the fore and the match flows. The more rounds, the more strategy matters and the fewer kicks there are. The number can be changed during the match only if both teams agree.
41|2 teams, 2 or more players
42|There are always two teams on the pitch, but a team can be run by more than one person. Teammates take turns: one person runs the team until the kick is taken, and in the move cycle after the kick the next person takes over until the following kick. So the game can be played one against one, in pairs or with larger teams.
43|Card values
44|On the lower back of every player piece there are three numbers showing the maximum number of squares it can move.
45|Blue number
46|Positioning
47|When the opponent has the ball, the maximum number of squares any of your players can move on your turn.
48|Green number
49|Dribbling
50|The maximum number of squares your player in possession can move with the ball.
51|Red number
52|Off-the-Ball Run
53|When you have the ball, the maximum number of squares your players other than the one in possession can move.
54|Match flow
55|Setup
56|The pitch is placed on a completely flat surface such as a table or the floor. Agree on the number of move rounds and on which optional rules apply.
57|Coin toss and line-up
58|The winner of the coin toss decides who takes the kick-off. The player taking the kick-off places their pieces on the squares anywhere on the pitch; then the opponent also lines up freely.
59|Kick
60|The team in possession takes its kick. You may strike the ball with any part of the player piece. The moment a player piece touches the ball, the kick counts as taken.
61|Who has the ball
62|The player nearest to the square where the ball stops gains possession and is moved to the ball. The ball is not moved; the piece is.
63|The team in possession moves first: either an off-the-ball run or a dribble. When the turn passes to the opponent, they move any one of their players using its positioning value. Either side can say “pass” instead of moving and hand over the turn.
64|Kick again
65|When the agreed move rounds are over, the team in possession takes a kick and the loop starts again.
66|Who has the ball?
67|Distance is the smallest number of squares a player needs to reach the ball. Ties are broken in this order:
68|Distance
69|The player who reaches the ball in the fewest squares.
70|Overall skill
71|If the distance is equal, the player with the higher overall skill.
72|Last kick
73|If both are equal, the player of the team that kicked the ball last.
74|Special cases
75|The kicker
76|The player who took the kick cannot regain the ball even if nearest to it; that player is ignored in the calculation.
77|Ball out of play
78|If the ball leaves the pitch, the opposing player nearest to where it went out gains possession and the ball is placed on that player's square. Even if the ball hits an opponent on the way out, it counts as put out by the kicking team.
79|Goalkeeper
80|When working out possession, the goalkeeper is treated as standing on the square inside the six-yard box on the side nearest the ball, and the distance is counted from that square. If this gives possession to the goalkeeper, the goalkeeper's nearest teammate gains the ball and it is placed on that player's square.
81|Shots and handling the goalkeeper
82|A player who decides to shoot at goal must tell the opponent before kicking. The opponent uses the rod behind the goalkeeper to set its position; once they let go of the rod they may not touch the goalkeeper again and it stays where it is. Unlike table football, there is no spinning or moving the goalkeeper during the shot: the goalkeeper is positioned first, then the shot is taken.
83|Line decisions
84|For goals and out-of-play decisions the whole ball must cross the line: seen from above, it must not touch the line.
85|Optional rules
86|These are optional. Teams must agree before the match which of them apply.
87|Ball Steal
88|Strategy +
89|If a player of the team without the ball can reach the square of the opponent in possession using its positioning power and has a higher overall skill, it steals the ball and makes its move starting from any square next to the one where the ball was won.
90|If its overall skill is lower, +3 is added to overall skill for every unit of positioning power left over after reaching the opponent. Example: a player with positioning power 5 and skill 80 who reaches the opponent on the 3rd square counts as 86.
91|If the skills are equal after the calculation, the attempt fails and the move is made again.
92|Brings strategic and arithmetic thinking to the fore; slows the flow because moves take longer.
93|No Goals from Own Half
94|Flow +
95|If the player in possession kicks from behind the halfway line, a goal does not count and the kick is retaken.
96|Reduces how often the ball goes out and makes the game flow; makes scoring harder.
97|Offside
98|Realism +
99|If a player of the kicking team is 1 or more squares ahead of the row of the rearmost opposing player, that player is ignored when working out possession.
100|Lowers the number of goals and makes for a more realistic match.
101|Our Throw-In
102|If the ball hits a player and goes out over a touchline, the throw-in goes to the opponent of the team it hit. The ball is placed on the square of the player of the team awarded the throw-in who is nearest to where it went out.
103|Can lead to “it touched / it didn't touch” arguments between teams.
104|Three Corners, One Penalty
105|Attack +
106|If the ball hits an opposing player and goes out over the goal line, it counts as a corner; corners are not taken, and 3 corners earn 1 penalty.
107|During a penalty, defenders in front of the goal are removed temporarily and put back on the same squares after the kick.
108|Special Cards Count
109|Collection
110|Special cards, available only to those who complete the collection, are included in the game and give their owner an advantage.
111|The match is fairer when both sides have these cards.
112|Pass Requirement
113|Before the game, teams agree on a number x. To earn the right to shoot at goal, a team must complete at least x accurate passes in a row without losing the ball to the opponent.
114|The number x is set by mutual agreement.
